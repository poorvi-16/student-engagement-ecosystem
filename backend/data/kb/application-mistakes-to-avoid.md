## Starting too late
Strong applications take months: tests, a statement of purpose, recommendation letters, transcripts and fees. Many deadlines are in the winter for the next autumn, and scholarships and housing close earlier still. Work backwards from your earliest deadline.

## One generic statement of purpose
Admissions teams read hundreds of statements. A generic essay with the university's name swapped in is easy to spot. Say why this program, which courses or labs, and how your projects led here. Use specifics and numbers, and avoid cliches like "since childhood".

## Weak recommenders
Pick people who know your work well and can give examples, not the most senior name. Give them your CV, your goals and the deadlines at least a month ahead, and follow up politely.

## Ignoring test requirements
Check each program's requirement for GRE, GMAT and English tests, minimum section scores and validity dates. Some programs are test-optional but still reward a good score. Book test dates early because slots fill up.

## Inconsistent facts
Dates, grades, employers and project titles must match across your CV, essays, transcripts and visa forms. Inconsistencies cause rejections and later visa problems.

## Not planning the money
Applying without a funding plan leads to panic after the offer. Estimate cost with the ROI calculator, check scholarships, and talk to your family about a co-applicant and collateral early. Knowing your funding gap before you apply makes the loan process smoother later.
