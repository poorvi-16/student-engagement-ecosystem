## What a Statement of Purpose should do
An SOP explains why you want this specific program, what has prepared you for it and what you will do with it. Admissions committees read it to judge motivation, clarity of goals and fit. Typical length is 800–1,000 words unless the university says otherwise.

## A simple SOP structure
1) A specific opening: a problem or project that made you choose this field (not a childhood story or a quote). 2) Academic preparation: relevant courses, projects, research, with results. 3) Work or internship experience: what you built or changed, with numbers. 4) Why this program: name courses, labs, faculty or specialisations that match your goals. 5) Career goals: a concrete short-term role and a long-term direction. 6) A brief close that ties it together.

## Common SOP mistakes
Repeating the CV line by line, generic praise of the university, vague goals ("I want to make an impact"), unexplained gaps or a low semester, using the same SOP for every university, and copied or AI-generated text that sounds generic. Universities increasingly run plagiarism and AI-detection checks, so use AI tools for feedback, not to write the whole essay.

## Letters of Recommendation (LORs)
Most programs ask for 2–3 LORs. Choose recommenders who know your work well: professors who taught or guided you in projects, and managers for work experience. Give them your CV, transcript, SOP draft and a short list of specific things you did with them, and ask at least 4–6 weeks before the deadline. Many universities send online recommendation forms directly to the recommender's email.

## Resume for applications
One to two pages, reverse chronological, with measurable achievements (e.g. "cut API latency by 40%"). Put projects, publications, internships, technical skills and test scores clearly. Tailor it for MS (projects, research) versus MBA (leadership, impact).
