## How the study permit works
With a Letter of Acceptance from a Designated Learning Institution (DLI) you apply online to IRCC, give biometrics and usually take a medical exam. Canada caps international study permits; many applicants need a Provincial or Territorial Attestation Letter (PAL/TAL), so check whether your program requires one. The Student Direct Stream (SDS) fast track ended in November 2024, so Indian students now use the regular process.

## Proof of funds
You must show first-year tuition, return travel and living costs. For applications from 1 September 2026, the living-cost amount for a single applicant outside Quebec is CAD 23,448 (Quebec sets its own amount). Accepted proof includes bank statements, a GIC, an education loan from a financial institution, or scholarships.

## Working while studying
Eligible full-time students can work off campus up to 24 hours a week during academic sessions and full-time during scheduled breaks.

## Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP)
Graduates of eligible programs can get an open work permit. Master's graduates generally get up to 3 years regardless of program length. Bachelor's, master's and doctoral graduates are exempt from the field-of-study requirement, which mainly affects diplomas and certificates (the eligible list is frozen for 2026). University graduates must show language test results at CLB 7 when applying for the PGWP.

## Permanent residence
Canadian work experience on a PGWP can lead to permanent residence through Express Entry (Canadian Experience Class) or provincial programs. Draws and cut-offs change frequently, so treat PR as possible but not guaranteed when estimating ROI.
