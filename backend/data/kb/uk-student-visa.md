## How the UK Student visa works
Once you accept an unconditional offer and pay any required deposit, the university issues a CAS (Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies). You apply online on GOV.UK, pay the visa fee and the Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS), and give biometrics at a VFS centre in India. Decisions usually take about 3 weeks; priority services are available. Check current fees on GOV.UK because they rise most years.

## Money you must show (maintenance funds)
You must show course fees still owed for the first year plus living costs for up to 9 months. As of late 2025 this is £1,529 per month in London and £1,171 per month outside London. A rise to about £1,570 / £1,203 has been announced for applications from 30 November 2026, so confirm the current figure. The money must be held for 28 consecutive days and the statement must be no more than 31 days old when you apply. An education loan sanction letter from a regulated lender is accepted as evidence.

## Dependants
Since January 2024 most students on taught master's courses cannot bring dependants (spouse or children). Research programs (PhD, research master's) are exempt.

## Working while studying
Degree-level students can usually work up to 20 hours a week during term and full-time in official vacations. Check the work conditions on your eVisa.

## Graduate Route (post-study work)
The Graduate visa lets you stay and work without a sponsor after your degree. It is currently 2 years for bachelor's and master's graduates (3 years for PhD). For applications made from 1 January 2027 it is being reduced to 18 months for bachelor's and master's graduates; PhD graduates keep 3 years. If you plan a one-year UK master's, factor the shorter stay into your job-search plan and ROI.

## English requirement
Universities set their own English scores (often IELTS 6.5 overall with no band below 6.0 for master's). Some accept a Medium of Instruction letter from Indian universities, but many still ask for IELTS, TOEFL or PTE.
