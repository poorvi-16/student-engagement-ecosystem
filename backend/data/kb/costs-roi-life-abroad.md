## What the total cost includes
Tuition for the full program, living costs (rent, food, transport, phone), health insurance, visa and test fees, flights, a laptop and initial setup (deposit, furniture). Rent is usually the biggest living cost; big cities such as New York, London, Toronto, Sydney and Dublin cost much more than smaller university towns.

## Thinking about ROI
A simple way to judge return on investment: compare the total cost in rupees with the realistic first-year salary after graduation in that country and field, and estimate how many years of savings it would take to repay it. Look at the post-study work period (OPT, Graduate Route, PGWP, 485, Stamp 1G, German job-seeker permit), because it decides how long you can earn in that currency. Use median salaries, not the best-case numbers in university brochures, and include a scenario where you return to India.

## Part-time work while studying
Part-time jobs help with daily expenses but rarely cover tuition. Typical limits: USA on-campus 20 hours a week; UK 20 hours a week in term; Canada 24 hours a week off campus; Australia 48 hours per fortnight; Germany 140 full days per year; Ireland 20 hours a week in term. Do not plan your budget on part-time income for the first semester.

## Reducing costs
Choose STEM or one-year programs where they suit your goals, apply for scholarships and assistantships, share accommodation, compare forex rates, and pay the moratorium interest on your loan during studies if you can, which can reduce your EMI noticeably.
