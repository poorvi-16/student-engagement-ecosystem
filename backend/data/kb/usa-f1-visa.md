## How the F-1 process works
After you accept an admit, the university issues a Form I-20 once you show proof of funds for the first year (tuition + living, as listed on the I-20). Then you pay the SEVIS I-901 fee, fill the DS-160 online form, pay the visa application (MRV) fee, and book biometrics (OFC) and the interview at a US consulate in India (Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, Hyderabad or Kolkata). Fees change often, so check the current amounts on the US visa website before paying.

## Proof of funds for the I-20 and visa
Funds can be shown as savings, fixed deposits, an education loan sanction letter, scholarships or sponsor income. A loan sanction letter from a bank or NBFC is widely accepted and is often the main proof for Indian students. Consular officers also look at whether the funding story is consistent and whether you plan to return after studies.

## Visa interview tips
Interviews are short (often 2–3 minutes). Be ready to explain: why this program and university, how it fits your past studies or work, who is funding you and how, and your plans after graduation. Answer in your own words; memorised answers are easy to spot. Since 2025 applicants have been asked to keep social-media accounts public for vetting.

## Period of stay: end of "duration of status"
From 15 September 2026 a DHS final rule replaces open-ended "duration of status" with a fixed admission period for most new F-1 students (generally the program length, capped at four years). Extensions are requested from USCIS instead of only through the university. Transition rules apply to students already in the US. Check the Federal Register / Study in the States for the latest details, including the grace period after graduation.

## Working during and after studies
On-campus work is allowed up to 20 hours a week during term. Curricular Practical Training (CPT) allows internships that are part of the curriculum. Optional Practical Training (OPT) gives up to 12 months of work after graduation; graduates of STEM-designated programs can apply for a 24-month STEM OPT extension (up to 3 years total), which is why STEM designation matters for ROI. OPT has faced political proposals to restrict it, so check its current status before relying on it.

## After OPT: H-1B
Most long-term US careers move from OPT to the H-1B work visa, which is employer-sponsored and selected by lottery with an annual cap. Treat H-1B as uncertain when calculating ROI; a realistic plan includes a fallback (for example returning to India or moving to another country with your US degree and experience).
