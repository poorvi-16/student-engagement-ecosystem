## Ireland: study visa
Indian students need a long-stay study (D) visa. You show the offer letter, tuition payment, private medical insurance and proof of funds for living costs (check the current minimum on the Irish Immigration website, as it was raised in recent years). After arrival you register with immigration and get Stamp 2 permission.

## Ireland: work and post-study (Stamp 1G)
Students can work up to 20 hours a week in term and 40 hours in holidays (around June–September and mid-December to mid-January). Graduates of a Level 9 master's can usually get up to 2 years on the Third Level Graduate Programme (Stamp 1G) to look for work. Ireland hosts many tech and pharma companies, which makes it a common one-year master's option for Indian students.

## Netherlands: residence permit
For non-EU students the university applies for the residence permit (and entry visa) on your behalf as a recognised sponsor. You must show the funds the IND sets each year for living costs and pay tuition (non-EU fees are typically €10,000–€20,000+ per year). Many programs are taught in English.

## Netherlands: work and the orientation year
Students can work limited hours (typically up to 16 hours a week, or full-time in June–August) with a work permit obtained by the employer. After graduation the orientation year (zoekjaar) permit lets you live and work freely for up to one year while you look for a job, after which you can switch to a highly skilled migrant permit with a lower salary threshold for recent graduates.
