## Why Germany is popular with Indian students
Most public universities charge no tuition for master's programs; you pay a semester contribution (typically around €150–€400 per semester, often including a public-transport ticket). The state of Baden-Württemberg charges non-EU students about €1,500 per semester. Many engineering and computer science master's are taught in English.

## APS certificate (mandatory for Indian students)
Indian applicants must get an APS certificate (Academic Evaluation Centre at the German Embassy, New Delhi) verifying their degree before applying for the student visa, and many universities ask for it at application time. Processing can take several weeks, so apply early.

## Blocked account (proof of funds)
For the student visa you usually show a blocked account (Sperrkonto) holding the required yearly amount: €11,904 per year (€992 per month) as of 2026. You can withdraw only the monthly amount after arriving. Some consulates accept a scholarship or a formal obligation letter instead. Confirm the current amount with the embassy before transferring money.

## Applying: uni-assist and deadlines
Many universities use uni-assist for international applications. Typical deadlines are about 15 July for the winter semester (starting October) and about 15 January for the summer semester (starting April), but each program sets its own dates. German master's programs often check how closely your bachelor's courses match their requirements, plus your grade converted with the modified Bavarian formula.

## Working and staying after graduation
Students can work 140 full days or 280 half days per year. After graduating you can get a residence permit of up to 18 months to look for a job matching your qualification, then move to an EU Blue Card or work permit. Learning German (B1 or higher) greatly improves job prospects outside tech.
