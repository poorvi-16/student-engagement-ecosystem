## Section 80E deduction on education loan interest
The person who took the loan (student or parent) can deduct the full interest paid on an education loan for higher studies from taxable income under the old tax regime. There is no upper limit on the amount. The deduction is available for the year repayment starts and the next 7 years (8 years in total), and only for loans from banks, notified financial institutions or approved charitable institutions. Only interest qualifies, not the principal. It is not available under the new tax regime, so compare both regimes once you start earning.

## Sending money abroad (LRS)
Under the RBI Liberalised Remittance Scheme, a resident individual can remit up to USD 250,000 per financial year for permitted purposes including education. Tuition is usually paid by wire transfer from a bank or through a forex provider directly to the university.

## TCS on education remittances
Tax Collected at Source (TCS) applies to large foreign remittances. For education, the threshold is ₹10 lakh per financial year. Remittances funded by an education loan from a financial institution attract no TCS. Self-funded education remittances above ₹10 lakh attract TCS on the amount above the threshold (check the current rate with your bank, as rules were revised in 2025–26). TCS is not an extra cost: it is credited against your income tax and refunded if you owe less.

## Forex costs
Banks and forex providers add a margin to the exchange rate plus a transfer fee. A difference of 1–2% in the rate on a ₹40 lakh tuition payment is ₹40,000–₹80,000, so compare the final rupee amount rather than the advertised rate.
