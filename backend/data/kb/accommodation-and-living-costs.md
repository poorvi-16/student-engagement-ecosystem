## Housing options
Most students choose between university halls, shared private flats and homestays. Halls are the easiest first year: they are close to campus, bills are often included and you meet people. Shared flats are usually cheaper per person but need a deposit, a lease and sometimes a local guarantor. Homestays suit some students and some countries' schemes.

## When to book
Apply for university housing as soon as you accept your offer, because places in popular cities fill early. Private rentals are tight in London, Dublin, Amsterdam and many Australian and Canadian cities, so start looking two to three months ahead and book a temporary stay for the first weeks if needed.

## What a monthly budget includes
Rent, food, local transport, phone and internet, utilities, health cover (sometimes mandatory and paid upfront), books and course materials, and a small amount for leisure. Big cities cost much more than smaller university towns. Use a cost-of-living tool for your exact city, and add a buffer of 10 to 15 percent for rupee movement and surprises.

## One-time costs people forget
Security deposit, first month's rent, flights, visa and health surcharges, bedding and kitchen basics, a winter coat, and a laptop if yours is old. The ROI calculator on PathPilot includes a one-time cost estimate so your funding gap is not understated.

## Scams to avoid
Never pay a deposit for a flat you have not seen or checked through a platform or the university. Be careful with listings that ask for payment by gift card or transfer to an individual, and verify the landlord through the housing office when you can.
