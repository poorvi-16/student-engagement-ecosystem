## Paying tuition and living costs from India
Education remittances go through authorised dealer banks under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme. You will need the university's invoice or offer letter, your PAN, and the bank's remittance form. A loan disbursed directly to the university means you do not have to move large sums yourself.

## TCS and tax
Tax collected at source applies to many foreign remittances, with different treatment for education paid out of an education loan from a notified institution. Rates and thresholds change, so check the current rule on the income tax site and ask your bank before you remit. TCS can generally be claimed as credit when you file your return.

## Getting a good exchange rate
Banks add a margin and fees to the mid-market rate. Compare the all-in cost (rate plus fees) across your bank, forex cards and specialist transfer services, and avoid many small transfers if each carries a fixed fee. Ask whether the university accepts payment through a partner platform that can lower the cost.

## Forex cards and local accounts
A prepaid forex card helps for the first weeks. Once you have your address and student ID, open a local student bank account and move your monthly spending there. Many banks let you open one before you arrive or in the first week, and some offer fee-free student accounts.

## Keeping records
Keep invoices, bank advices and receipts. They support your visa funding proof, your tax return and your loan documents, and a clean paper trail makes later lender checks easier.
