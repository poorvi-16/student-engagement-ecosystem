## Fit before rank
Rankings are a starting point. A strong fit means the curriculum covers what you want to learn, there are faculty or labs in your area, and graduates land the kind of jobs you want. A lower-ranked program with good industry links can beat a famous name that does not teach your specialisation.

## Build a balanced list
Aim for a mix: a few ambitious programs where your profile is below the typical admit, several targets where it matches, and a couple of safe options. PathPilot's Navigator buckets programs this way and shows the estimated chance and the total cost for each.

## Look at outcomes, not brochures
Check employment rate, median salary and where graduates work, using the university's reports and LinkedIn alumni search. Compare the cost against the salary you can realistically expect, which is what the ROI calculator does with payback time.

## STEM and work rights matter
In several countries post-study work length or extensions depend on the degree being in an eligible field. This can change the value of a program by years of work rights, so check it for each country.

## Practical filters
Duration (one-year versus two-year masters), intake and deadlines, test requirements (GRE, GMAT, IELTS or TOEFL), scholarships, and the city's cost of living. Contact current students and ask about workload, teaching quality and support. Two honest conversations teach you more than a ranking table.
