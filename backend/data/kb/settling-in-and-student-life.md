## The first month
Attend orientation, meet your department, register with a doctor or campus health service, and sort out your bank, SIM, transport pass and student ID. Many universities run welcome weeks for international students. Use them to find friends and clubs.

## Academic culture
Teaching often expects more independent reading, group projects and class participation than many Indian courses. Academic integrity rules are strict: always cite sources and never share or reuse assignments. Use the writing centre, office hours and peer study groups early, before problems pile up.

## Mental wellbeing
Homesickness, money worry and workload are common. Most universities offer free counselling and peer support for international students. Keeping a routine, sleep, exercise and regular calls home help. Reaching out early is a sign of strength.

## Making friends and networking
Join clubs, society events and volunteering, and mix with local and international students. Attend career fairs and alumni talks. Professional connections made in your first year often lead to internships.

## Safety and local rules
Learn the local emergency numbers, public transport safety and tenancy rules. Respect visa conditions about work hours and attendance, because breaking them can affect your status. Keep your documents safe and register with the Indian embassy or consulate if your country has a scheme for it.
